# ECP 90-Day Rollout: Deep Analysis & Leadership Presentation
**Sr. Lead, P&T Operations — EnterpriseVendor**
**Author:** Vignesh S | Date: 2026-06-30 | Version: 1.0

---

## Executive Summary

The EnterpriseVendor Customer Collaboration Program (ECP) is not a process rollout — it is a **perception shift**. The Product Management team currently experiences launch obligations as compliance overhead. The 90-day mandate is to convert that perception into a lived conviction: that structured customer collaboration *accelerates* launches, reduces rework, and builds the kind of evidence base that wins internal prioritisation.

This document provides the deep analytical foundation for a 5-slide leadership presentation covering four pillars:
1. Change Management & Adoption Strategy
2. Rollout Execution (phased, pilot-first)
3. Enablement & Support Model
4. Success Measurement Framework

---

## Part 1: Change Management & Adoption Strategy

### 1.1 Root Cause of Resistance (Why PMs Push Back)

Before designing adoption strategy, it is critical to name the real resistances — not the stated ones:

| Stated Objection | Actual Fear | Evidence Proxy |
|-----------------|-------------|----------------|
| "We don't have time" | ECP will slow sprint velocity | PMs track story throughput religiously |
| "Our customers are different" | Generic frameworks won't fit our segment | Past failed tools that didn't map to deal complexity |
| "We already talk to customers" | Informal channels feel safer / less auditable | Ad hoc Slack threads with CS vs. structured session notes |
| "Leadership will change the priorities anyway" | Effort won't lead to outcome they control | Prior roadmap resets post-customer research |
| "Who owns the output?" | Ambiguity on accountability creates avoidance | Undefined RACI in current program touchpoints |

**Insight:** Resistance is not laziness — it is rational risk aversion under high uncertainty. The change strategy must reduce perceived personal risk, not mandate compliance.

### 1.2 The Perception Reframe: Bottleneck → Launch Accelerator

The central narrative shift required:

> **Old story:** "ECP is a gate you pass through before you can ship."
> **New story:** "ECP is the evidence kit that lets you ship faster, fight for budget, and win stakeholder approval without re-explaining."

This reframe has three supporting proof points that must be established in the first 30 days:

**Proof Point 1 — Velocity.** Show that PMs who run structured customer sessions resolve acceptance criteria ambiguity 40% faster (benchmark from similar CSM programs at B2B SaaS companies). Framing: "You spend 3 hours in a structured session to save 2 sprints of rework."

**Proof Point 2 — Political capital.** ECP outputs (NPS deltas, verbatim quotes, feature impact scores) give PMs defensible evidence when fighting for roadmap priority in planning cycles. Framing: "Your gut feel becomes a data point. Your customer quote becomes a board-ready slide."

**Proof Point 3 — Career signal.** PMs who generate structured customer evidence are the ones who get promoted at product-led companies. Framing: "This is how top-1% PMs operate. We're building you the infrastructure."

### 1.3 Stakeholder Map & Buy-In Sequence

Buy-in must be sequenced — not broadcast. Wrong order creates noise; right order creates momentum.

```
Week 1-2:  Sponsor alignment (CPO / VP Product)
            └── Non-negotiable: they must name ECP outputs as a planning gate input
Week 2-3:  Early adopter PMs (2-3 most customer-curious)
            └── They become internal evangelists, not top-down enforcers
Week 3-4:  CS + Sales Leaders
            └── ECP needs their customer access; frame as mutual benefit
Week 4-6:  Wider PO team (14+ PMs)
            └── By now, 2-3 pilots have early wins to share
Week 6-8:  Engineering Partners
            └── Show them how ECP reduces scope creep on their side
```

**Critical:** Do NOT launch to the full PO team in week 1. Pilots first. Social proof is the strongest adoption driver in professional networks.

### 1.4 The "ECP Concierge" Model

The single most important change management lever: **remove the activation energy.**

A dedicated ECP Concierge role (can be the Sr. Lead P&T Operations in the first 90 days) owns:
- Session scheduling with CS / account teams
- Template preparation (agenda, note-taking framework, scoring rubrics)
- Post-session synthesis into structured outputs
- Pushing outputs into AgileBoard / WikiBoard so PMs don't context-switch

**Why this works:** PMs will adopt tools that reduce their cognitive load. If the first experience of ECP feels like "someone took care of the logistics and handed me a deliverable," the second session will be voluntary.

## Part 2: Rollout Execution — Phased Approach (90 Days)

### Strategic Choice: Pilot-First, Not Mandate-First

**Decision rationale:**

| Option | Pro | Con | Verdict |
|--------|-----|-----|---------|
| Mandate full rollout Day 1 | Fast coverage | Resistance hardens, compliance-only behavior, no genuine adoption | REJECTED |
| Voluntary opt-in forever | Low resistance | Never reaches critical mass, creates two-tier PO culture | REJECTED |
| Pilot → Expand → Embed | Evidence-driven expansion, generates internal evangelists, allows process refinement before scale | Slower initial coverage | SELECTED |

**Pilot selection criteria:**
1. PO is customer-curious (not necessarily most senior)
2. Product area has active CSM relationship with 3+ referenceable accounts
3. Upcoming launch or major feature decision in weeks 30-60 (so ECP output is immediately usable)
4. Domain complexity is medium (not the most complex product area — want first wins to be clean)

### Phase 1: Listen (Days 1–30) — "Find the signal"

**Objective:** Understand the current state, establish trust, identify the 2-3 highest-leverage pilot use cases.

| Action | Owner | Output |
|--------|-------|--------|
| 1:1 interviews with all PMs (20 min each) | Sr. Lead P&T | Pain point map, resistance typology, 3 pilot candidates |
| Audit existing customer touchpoints | Sr. Lead P&T + CS | Gap map: what customer signal exists, what's missing |
| Align with CPO/VP on ECP as planning input | Sr. Lead P&T | Sponsor memo: ECP evidence required in QBR decks by Day 90 |
| Draft ECP playbook v0.1 (not final) | Sr. Lead P&T | 8-page playbook: roles, templates, session formats, outputs |
| Map 3 pilot PMs to 3 customer accounts | Sr. Lead P&T + CS | Pilot charter: PO + account + session date confirmed |

**Day 30 checkpoint:** 3 pilots scoped, playbook drafted, sponsor aligned, no PO has been mandated — only invited.

### Phase 2: Pilot (Days 31–60) — "Prove the value"

**Objective:** Run 3 structured ECP sessions, generate real outputs, create internal social proof.

| Action | Owner | Output |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Run ECP Session 1 (PO-1 + Account A) | Concierge + PO-1 | Session notes, feature impact scores, verbatim quote bank |
| Run ECP Session 2 (PO-2 + Account B) | Concierge + PO-2 | JTBD synthesis, 3 actionable backlog items identified |
| Run ECP Session 3 (PO-3 + Account C) | Concierge + PO-3 | Roadmap risk flag surfaced: customer expectation vs. planned scope |
| Post-session synthesis for each | Concierge | Structured WikiBoard page per session (standardised format) |
| PO Retrospective: what worked / what didn't | All 3 pilot PMs | Playbook v0.2 with real learnings baked in |
| "Early Wins" internal comms | Sr. Lead P&T | 1-slide summary shared in PO all-hands: what ECP found |

**Day 60 checkpoint:** 3 sessions completed, 3 WikiBoard pages live, 1 backlog item per session actioned, playbook v0.2 published, 3 PMs are now voluntary advocates.

**Key risk at Phase 2:** Synthesis quality. If post-session outputs are messy, PMs will not trust the process. Invest heavily in synthesis templates. Each WikiBoard page must answer: What did we learn? What should we build? What should we stop building? What is the customer's emotional state?

### Phase 3: Scale (Days 61–90) — "Embed and measure"

**Objective:** Expand to full PO team, embed ECP into the operating rhythm, hand off to PMs from Concierge.

| Action | Owner | Output |
|--------|-------|--------|
| PO-wide ECP training workshop (90 min) | Sr. Lead P&T | All PMs trained on session facilitation, note-taking, scoring |
| ECP embedded in sprint planning template | Sr. Lead P&T + EM | "Customer evidence" section added to sprint review template |
| Assign each PO their own ECP account pairing | Sr. Lead P&T + CS | 8-10 PO-to-account pairings established |
| Sessions 4-10 run (remaining PMs) | Individual PMs (Concierge available) | Expanded evidence base; PMs begin facilitating independently |
| ECP dashboard live | Sr. Lead P&T | Metrics visible to leadership: sessions run, issues surfaced, actions taken |
| 90-Day review deck to CPO | Sr. Lead P&T | Evidence-based assessment: what worked, what to adjust, ask for Q2 resources |

**Day 90 checkpoint:** Full PO team has run or is scheduled for their first session; ECP outputs feeding into Q3 planning cycle; metrics dashboard live.

## Part 3: Enablement & Support Model

### 3.1 The Four-Layer Enablement Stack

```
Layer 4 — EMBED (Day 60+)
  ECP baked into sprint ceremonies, planning gates, QBR decks
  PMs self-serve with templates; Concierge = escalation only

Layer 3 — PRACTICE (Day 30-60)
  Supervised live sessions; Concierge co-facilitates first session per PO
  Real-time coaching; post-session debrief (15 min)

Layer 2 — LEARN (Day 15-30)
  90-min workshop: "The ECP Method" — JTBD, session design, scoring
  Role-play simulation: PO + Concierge mock session
  Playbook issued; WikiBoard templates live

Layer 1 — AWARE (Day 1-15)
  All-hands introduction: reframe narrative (bottleneck → accelerator)
  1:1 discovery interviews: listen first, don't sell
  FAQ document: "What is ECP and what is it NOT"
```

### 3.2 Template Stack (What PMs Actually Get)

Every PO receives a working toolkit on Day 30:

| Template | Format | Purpose |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Session Agenda Template | WikiBoard | 45/60/90 min formats; pre-read, discussion guide, next steps |
| Note-Taking Framework | WikiBoard | JTBD capture, friction logging, sentiment scoring (1-5) |
| Feature Impact Scoring Rubric | WikiBoard | Customer-stated vs. inferred importance; effort vs. value matrix |
| Post-Session Synthesis Template | WikiBoard | Learnings → Actions → Backlog items → Quote bank |
| PO-to-Customer Pairing Matrix | AgileBoard | Which PO owns which account relationship |
| ECP Health Dashboard | WikiBoard/AgileBoard | Sessions run, open actions, NPS delta, feature adoption rate |

### 3.3 The Concierge Handoff Curve

The Sr. Lead P&T Ops starts as high-touch Concierge and progressively hands off:

```
Days 1-30:   Concierge does 80% of logistics. PO shows up and runs the call.
Days 31-60:  Concierge co-facilitates. PO leads. Concierge synthesises.
Days 61-75:  PO runs independently. Concierge reviews synthesis output.
Days 76-90:  PO fully self-sufficient. Concierge = quality check + escalation.
Day 91+:     Concierge role transitions to programme governance & metrics.
```

**Failure mode to watch:** If Concierge remains in high-touch mode beyond Day 60, dependency forms and the program doesn't scale. The explicit goal is to make the Concierge role redundant for session logistics by Day 90.

### 3.4 Resistance Playbook

| Resistance Type | PO Persona | Response Tactic |
|----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| "No time" | High-velocity PO | Show 45-min session format; offer to handle 100% of scheduling + prep |
| "My customers are too busy" | Enterprise PO | Work with CSM to embed ECP into existing QBR / EBR agenda |
| "I already know what they want" | Senior PO | Ask to share their evidence; offer ECP as validation, not discovery |
| "This won't change anything" | Cynical PO | Show Day 60 pilot output: "This surfaced a blocker 6 weeks before launch" |
| "What if the customer says something bad?" | Risk-averse PO | Frame: "Better to know now than at GA. ECP is pre-launch insurance." |

## Part 4: Measuring Initial Success

### 4.1 Metric Philosophy

> "Track leading indicators in the first 30 days. If you only measure NPS at Day 90, you'll discover failure too late to fix it."

The metrics framework has three layers:
- **Activity metrics** (are people doing the thing?)
- **Quality metrics** (is the thing being done well?)
- **Outcome metrics** (is the thing driving business results?)

Leadership sees all three. Activity metrics are internal; outcome metrics go in QBR decks.

### 4.2 The ECP Metrics Framework

#### Layer 1: Activity Metrics (Weekly tracking, Days 1-90)

| Metric | Target D30 | Target D60 | Target D90 | Source |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| ECP sessions scheduled | 3 | 8 | 15 | AgileBoard / Calendar |
| ECP sessions completed | 2 | 6 | 12 | WikiBoard session pages |
| PMs with ≥1 completed session | 3 | 6 | 10 | PO tracking sheet |
| Post-session synthesis pages created | 2 | 6 | 12 | WikiBoard |
| Backlog items created from ECP | 3 | 10 | 20 | AgileBoard |

#### Layer 2: Quality Metrics (Bi-weekly review)

| Metric | Definition | Target | Source |
|--------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Session quality score | PO self-rates session value 1-5 | ≥ 3.8 avg | PO survey |
| Synthesis completeness | % synthesis pages with all 4 sections filled | ≥ 80% | WikiBoard audit |
| Customer return rate | % customers willing to do a follow-up session | ≥ 70% | CS confirmation |
| Time-to-synthesis | Hours between session end and synthesis published | ≤ 48hr | WikiBoard timestamps |
| Backlog action rate | % ECP backlog items actioned within 1 sprint | ≥ 50% | AgileBoard sprint review |

#### Layer 3: Outcome Metrics (D60 and D90 reviews)

| Metric | Definition | Target D90 | Lagging/Leading | Business Link |
|--------|-----------|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| **PO satisfaction** ⭐ | PO self-report: "ECP helps me do my job better" (1-5) | ≥ 4.0 | Leading | Internal adoption sustainability |
| Feature adoption delta | % change in feature adoption for ECP-informed features vs. non-ECP | +15% | Lagging | Product-market fit |
| Rework rate reduction | % reduction in post-launch scope changes for ECP features | -20% | Lagging | Engineering efficiency |
| Customer NPS delta | NPS change for accounts in ECP program vs. control group | +8 pts | Lagging | Retention / expansion |
| Time-to-evidence | Days from "we need customer input" to "evidence in deck" | ≤ 5 days | Leading | Decision velocity |
| Escalation prevention | # of launch-blocking issues surfaced pre-GA via ECP | ≥ 3 | Outcome | Risk reduction |

### 4.3 The D90 Leadership Test

At Day 90, the CPO asks two questions. The program is succeeding if we can answer both with data:

**Q1: "Did ECP actually change what we built?"**
→ Answer: Yes. [X] backlog items were created from ECP that would not have existed otherwise. [Y] features were descoped based on customer feedback. [Z] launch-blocking issues were surfaced in advance.

**Q2: "Are PMs better at their jobs because of ECP?"**
→ Answer: Yes. PO satisfaction score is [X]. [Y] PMs voluntarily ran their second session without being asked. Time-to-evidence dropped from [X days] to [Y days].

### 4.4 Leading Indicator Early Warning System

If any of these signals appear before Day 60, escalate immediately:

| Red Flag | What It Signals | Intervention |
|----------|----------------|-------------|
| Session completion rate < 50% | Scheduling friction too high | Concierge takes over all logistics |
| PO satisfaction < 3.0 after first session | Session design not valuable | Redesign agenda; add more structured discussion prompts |
| < 1 backlog item per session | Synthesis not actionable | Add "3 specific actions" field to synthesis template; make it mandatory |
| Customer decline rate > 40% | CS not bought in / customer fatigue | Re-engage CS leadership; offer incentive for customer participation |
| No sponsor mention in planning cycle | Executive air cover fading | Request CPO to name ECP explicitly in next planning kickoff |

## Part 5: Slide Deck Framework (5 Slides)

### Slide 1: The Problem Reframed
**Title:** "We're not launching a process. We're building a competitive advantage."

- Current state: PMs treat customer collaboration as a gate, not a tool
- Cost of status quo: 23% of B2B SaaS post-launch rework is attributable to insufficient pre-launch customer validation (benchmark)
- The ask: 90 days to prove that structured customer evidence makes our PMs faster, not slower
- The frame: ECP is not compliance — it is the evidence kit that wins roadmap battles and retention conversations

### Slide 2: Change Strategy — Win Hearts Before Mandating Hands
**Title:** "Social proof before system mandate."

- Three-stage buy-in sequence: Sponsor → Early Adopters → Full Team
- ECP Concierge model: remove activation energy for first-time PMs
- Reframe tactic: from "gate" to "pre-launch insurance + roadmap ammunition"
- Key insight: resistance is rational risk aversion, not laziness — design the program to reduce personal risk first

### Slide 3: The 90-Day Plan
**Title:** "Listen → Pilot → Scale: evidence-driven expansion."

| Phase | Days | Focus | Gate |
|-------|------|-------|------|
| Listen | 1-30 | Pain mapping, pilot selection, sponsor alignment | 3 pilots scoped + sponsor memo signed |
| Pilot | 31-60 | 3 sessions, synthesis, social proof | 3 WikiBoard pages + 3 PO advocates |
| Scale | 61-90 | Full team training, independent facilitation, metrics dashboard | 10+ sessions, dashboard live |

- Why pilot-first: evidence beats mandate; first wins create internal evangelists
- Pilot selection: customer-curious PO + active CSM relationship + imminent launch decision

### Slide 4: Enablement — The Concierge Model
**Title:** "We make the first session impossible to fail."

- Four-layer stack: Aware → Learn → Practice → Embed
- Template toolkit: 6 ready-to-use WikiBoard templates live Day 30
- Concierge handoff curve: 80% logistics support in Month 1 → PO fully independent by Day 90
- Resistance playbook: specific responses for 5 PO resistance personas
- Failure mode: don't let Concierge dependency persist past Day 60

### Slide 5: Success Metrics — Leading Before Lagging
**Title:** "We'll know by Day 45 if this is working."

**Leading indicators (tracked weekly):**
- Sessions completed on schedule
- PO satisfaction after first session (target: ≥ 3.8/5)
- Time-to-synthesis ≤ 48 hours

**Outcome indicators (D90 QBR):**
- ⭐ PO satisfaction ≥ 4.0 ("ECP makes me better at my job")
- Feature adoption delta +15% for ECP-informed features
- ≥ 3 launch-blocking issues surfaced pre-GA

**The D90 ask of leadership:**
- Resource: 0.5 FTE Concierge support confirmed for Q3
- Signal: CPO names ECP evidence as required input in Q3 planning kickoff
- Decision: Based on D90 data, expand to all product lines or course-correct

## Appendix A: Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| CS team not scheduling customer access | High | Critical | SLA with CS leadership: 5 business days to confirmed session |
| PO satisfaction scores low after pilot | Medium | High | Redesign session agenda; reduce from 60 to 45 min; add more structured prompts |
| Sponsor deprioritises mid-program | Medium | Critical | Embed ECP output as required field in planning template — structural, not dependent on sponsor attention |
| Customer fatigue / low participation | Low | High | Rotate accounts; integrate into existing QBR/EBR rhythm rather than creating new touchpoints |
| Synthesis quality inconsistent | High | Medium | Mandatory template + Concierge quality check for first 3 sessions per PO |
| Engineering team ignores ECP outputs | Medium | High | Embed ECP evidence into story definition-of-ready; requires PO + EM joint agreement |

## Appendix B: What "Good" Looks Like at Day 90

A PO who has gone through ECP properly should be able to say:

> "I ran two sessions with two enterprise accounts before this sprint. I walked in knowing what they're struggling with, what they've asked for and not gotten, and what they're planning to do if we don't ship this. My sprint goal is written in their language. My acceptance criteria came from their actual workflow. If this feature underperforms at launch, I'll know within a week because I'm tracking the three things they said they'd do differently."

That is the outcome. The 90-day plan is the path to it.
